# Official artwork licensing

Policy adopted: 2026-11-05. Maintainer: hobby-programmer-5000. English is authoritative.

## Which terms apply?

| Material | Terms |
| --- | --- |
| Original app code, including generation, rendering and motion logic | Unmodified [MIT](../../LICENSE) |
| Existing default character, random/Qwen vector variants and previously MIT-published material | Existing permissions remain; this policy adds no noncommercial restriction |
| Future official character artwork or sample images explicitly listed below | CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, for the rights the licensor can grant in those identified assets |
| Independently generated user artwork | Not automatically covered by the official-artwork restriction; check the model terms and any underlying material |
| Third-party models, libraries or artwork | Their respective [licenses](THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.md) |

The repository's MIT license is not amended with a noncommercial clause. Earlier MIT permissions, including commercial permissions, are preserved. Exporting, recoloring or renaming previously MIT-published material does not erase those earlier permissions. This policy does not declare all output from a renderer, model or example prompt to be noncommercial.

## Future designated artwork

The selected license is [Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial-ShareAlike 4.0 International](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-sa/4.0/); its [official legal code](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-sa/4.0/legalcode.en) governs designated assets. It permits noncommercial sharing and adaptation with attribution; shared adaptations must follow its ShareAlike terms. Commercial permissions are not granted by that license. It does not grant trademark rights or override legal exceptions, public-domain status or earlier permissions.

**Current designation register: no assets designated.** As of adoption, no existing character, drawing, prompt, screenshot or file is moved to CC BY-NC-SA. No new official artwork is created by this documentation change. Released 0.4.1 files keep their existing terms.

Before publishing a new designated asset, the maintainer must:

1. Confirm the rights that can actually be licensed and record human/AI contributions, source material and applicable model terms. Do not designate third-party content without authority.
2. Add an entry here identifying its title, exact repository file paths, SHA-256 values, first release/commit, publication date, licensor, attribution credit and any excluded elements. Update the Korean register to match.
3. Place a CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 notice beside the files, clearly distinguishing the artwork from MIT code. Include the unmodified official license text and attribution with every distributed asset package.
4. Preserve each asset version's record and any earlier grants. Treat a materially new asset separately; a new label cannot withdraw permissions already granted for existing material.

Do not call noncommercial assets OSI-approved open-source software. Code and designated artwork have separate terms even when packaged together. Follow the [CC licensing guidance](https://creativecommons.org/faq/) when preparing the notice.

## Examples and generated variants

The app's existing **Insert example** prompts remain under their existing terms. A prompt is not a specific rendered image. Selecting an example or generating a random character does not itself activate an official-asset restriction.

An adaptation that uses protected expression from a designated asset must respect the applicable asset license. Independently generated art, generic proportions, animal features, colors and styles are not claimed merely because they resemble a theme. AI output is not automatically copyrightable or exclusively owned; a license cannot create missing rights. See [character provenance and limits](CHARACTER-RIGHTS.md).

This policy is a licensing boundary, not a guarantee of exclusivity, infringement clearance or trademark registration.
